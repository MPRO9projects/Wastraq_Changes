# Website Content Optimization & Approval Guidelines

## Purpose

Before creating or redesigning any website page, every team member must first review the **existing approved content** for that page.

The existing content should **not** be copied directly into the new design without review.

The content must first be:

1. Read and understood completely
2. Simplified and shortened where possible
3. Restructured for better readability
4. Optimized for SEO
5. Optimized for AEO
6. Optimized for GEO
7. Checked for accuracy and consistency
8. Submitted for approval
9. Implemented only after approval

> **Do not publish or implement rewritten website content without approval.**

---

## Content Change Workflow

Every page must follow the workflow below.

### Step 1 — Review the Existing Content

Before changing anything, read the complete existing page content.

Understand:

- What the page is about
- Who the page is written for
- What product, service, or solution is being explained
- What information is essential
- What information is repetitive
- What information is outdated
- What claims are being made
- What action the visitor should take
- Which keywords or topics the page should target

Do not start rewriting individual sentences without understanding the complete page first.

---

### Step 2 — Identify the Main Search Intent

Determine what the visitor is likely trying to understand or accomplish.

Examples:

- Learn about a product
- Understand a solution
- Compare capabilities
- Request a demo
- Contact the company
- Understand how a feature works
- Find an answer to a specific problem

The final content must satisfy this intent clearly and quickly.

---

### Step 3 — Shorten the Existing Content

Reduce unnecessary content while preserving the original meaning.

Remove:

- Repeated statements
- Generic marketing language
- Long introductions
- Unnecessary technical explanations
- Duplicate benefits
- Filler sentences
- Excessive adjectives
- Repeated keywords

Prefer:

- Short paragraphs
- Clear statements
- Strong headings
- Bullet points
- Specific benefits
- Direct explanations

#### Rule

Do not shorten content by removing important information.

The objective is:

> **Less text + clearer meaning + stronger information.**

---

## Heading Structure

Keep the heading hierarchy consistent across every page.

```text
H1 → Main page topic
H2 → Major sections
H3 → Subsections
H4 → Only when genuinely required
```

Do not randomly jump between heading levels.

Example:

```text
H1: Smart Waste Management Platform

H2: Automate Waste Collection Operations
H3: Automatic Route Planning
H3: Smart Collection Alerts

H2: Improve Fleet Performance
H3: Real-Time Vehicle Monitoring
```

### Recommended Heading Length

| Heading | Recommended Length |
|---|---:|
| H1 | 5–12 words |
| H2 | 4–10 words |
| H3 | 3–8 words |
| H4 | 2–8 words |

Headings should describe the actual topic of the section.

Avoid vague headings such as:

- Our Solution
- Better Results
- Why Us
- Next Generation

Prefer descriptive headings such as:

- Automate Waste Collection Scheduling
- Monitor Fleet Performance in Real Time
- Reduce Missed Waste Collections

---

## SEO Content Requirements

Every rewritten page must be optimized for relevant search queries without making the content sound unnatural.

Before rewriting, identify:

- Primary keyword
- Secondary keywords
- Related terminology
- Search intent
- Important entities or topics

The primary keyword should naturally appear in:

1. SEO title
2. H1
3. First 100 words
4. At least one H2 where relevant
5. Meta description
6. URL where appropriate
7. Relevant image ALT text
8. Relevant internal-link anchor text

### Keyword Usage Rule

Do not target a fixed keyword density.

Use:

> **Primary keyword + related terms + natural variations**

Avoid repeating the same keyword in every paragraph.

#### Example

Instead of repeatedly writing:

> Waste management software helps companies manage waste. Our waste management software provides waste management software features...

Use natural variations such as:

- Waste management platform
- Collection management system
- Smart waste operations
- Waste collection technology
- Fleet and bin monitoring

---

## AEO Content Requirements

AEO means **Answer Engine Optimization**.

Content should make it easy for search engines, AI assistants, and answer engines to extract a clear answer.

For every important section:

- Answer the main question directly
- Put the answer in the first 1–2 sentences
- Use simple and natural language
- Use question-based headings when appropriate
- Clearly define technical terms
- Include relevant FAQs
- Avoid unnecessarily long paragraphs
- Use bullet points when listing information
- Use tables when comparing information

### Ideal Answer Length

| Answer Type | Recommended Length |
|---|---:|
| Direct answer | 40–80 words |
| Detailed explanation | 100–200 words |

#### Example

Avoid:

> With the rapid advancement of technology and increasing requirements within waste management operations, organizations are beginning to consider new approaches...

Prefer:

> Smart waste collection uses sensors, route optimization, and real-time vehicle data to help operators schedule collections more efficiently and reduce unnecessary trips.

The answer should appear immediately.

---

## GEO Content Requirements

GEO means **Generative Engine Optimization**.

Content should help generative AI systems clearly understand the company, page, solution, and topic.

Every important page should clearly explain:

- What the company or product is
- What the solution does
- Who it is for
- What problem it solves
- How it works
- What its main capabilities are
- Why those capabilities matter

Content should:

- Explain topics naturally
- Provide useful context
- Cover related subtopics
- Use consistent terminology
- Include specific facts where available
- Demonstrate practical expertise
- Explain real-world applications
- Avoid repetitive AI-style wording
- Avoid keyword stuffing

### Recommended Major Section Size

**100–250 words per important topic**

This is a guideline, not a requirement.

The goal is:

> **Complete topical coverage without unnecessary content.**

---

## Paragraph Length

Website content must be easy to scan.

### Recommended

**40–80 words per paragraph**

Prefer:

- 2–4 short paragraphs
- Bullet points
- Subheadings
- Highlighted facts
- Short supporting descriptions

Avoid large blocks containing 150–300 words unless absolutely necessary.

---

## Overall Page Length

There is no mandatory SEO word count.

Use only the amount of content required to answer the visitor's intent properly.

### General Guidelines

| Page Type | General Guideline |
|---|---:|
| Landing or Service Page | 800–1,500 words |
| Informational Page | 1,000–2,000 words |
| Detailed Pillar Page | 2,000–3,500+ words |

Do not increase content length simply to reach a word-count target.

A shorter page with complete and useful information is better than a long page filled with repetitive content.

---

## Content Accuracy

When optimizing existing content, do not change factual meaning simply to make the copy sound stronger.

Do not invent:

- Statistics
- Performance improvements
- Customer numbers
- Certifications
- Partnerships
- Product capabilities
- Awards
- Locations
- Case studies
- Testimonials

Claims must be supported by approved company information.

If a statement cannot be verified, flag it for review.

---

## CTA Requirements

Conversion-focused sections should have a clear next action.

| CTA Element | Recommended Length |
|---|---:|
| CTA Heading | 5–10 words |
| CTA Description | 20–40 words |
| CTA Button | 2–5 words |

Examples:

- Request a Demo
- Explore Solutions
- Contact Us
- Get Started
- Learn More
- Talk to Our Team

The CTA should match the purpose of the page.

Avoid placing unrelated CTAs simply because space is available.

---

## FAQ Requirements

Where FAQs genuinely help users, include:

**5–10 relevant questions**

Questions should represent actual user or search intent.

Use:

```text
H3 → Question
40–80 word direct answer
```

Example:

### How does smart waste route optimization work?

Smart route optimization analyzes collection requirements, bin status, vehicle availability, and route priorities to create more efficient collection schedules. Routes can be updated when operational conditions change.

Avoid creating FAQs only to repeat keywords.

---

## Internal Linking

Each page should contain approximately:

**3–8 relevant internal links**

Internal links should help visitors discover related information.

Use descriptive anchor text.

Avoid:

- Click here
- Read more
- More information

Prefer:

- Explore our waste management solutions
- Learn about smart route optimization
- View our fleet management capabilities
- Discover waste collection automation

---

## Image Content Requirements

Images also form part of the page's SEO and content quality.

Important website images should:

- Use WebP or AVIF where supported
- Be compressed
- Use appropriate dimensions
- Have descriptive filenames
- Include relevant ALT text
- Lazy-load below-the-fold assets

### ALT Text

Recommended length:

**5–15 words**

ALT text must describe what the image actually shows.

Example:

> Driver using WASTRAQ waste collection mobile application

Do not insert keywords into ALT text when they do not describe the image.

---

## Content Quality Standard

Every page should follow this principle:

> **Clear → Useful → Specific → Scannable → Trustworthy → Search-Intent Focused**

Before approval, ask:

- Is the page easy to understand?
- Can important information be found quickly?
- Is unnecessary content removed?
- Does the page clearly explain the topic?
- Is terminology consistent?
- Does the page satisfy the visitor's likely question?
- Are all claims accurate?
- Does the content sound natural?
- Does the page help both people and search/AI systems understand the topic?

---

## Content We Must Avoid

Do not use:

- Keyword stuffing
- Duplicate content
- Repeated sentences
- Generic filler
- Excessive marketing claims
- Very long paragraphs
- Unnecessary headings
- Unverified claims
- Artificial AI-sounding language
- Content written only for search engines
- Content added only to increase word count

> **Human readability always comes first.**

---

## Content Change Submission

After rewriting a page, do not directly replace the website content.

Prepare the content for review using the following structure.

### Existing Content

Include the current approved content.

### Proposed Content

Include the newly optimized version.

### Reason for Change

Briefly explain:

- What was shortened
- What was restructured
- What SEO improvements were made
- What AEO improvements were made
- What GEO improvements were made
- Whether headings changed
- Whether CTAs changed
- Whether any factual information requires verification

---

## Content Approval Process

Every changed page must go through approval before development or publication.

```text
Existing Content
      ↓
Content Review
      ↓
SEO + AEO + GEO Optimization
      ↓
Internal Content Review
      ↓
Content Approval
      ↓
Design / Development Implementation
      ↓
Final QA
      ↓
Publish
```

### Important Rule

> **No redesigned, shortened, or SEO-optimized content should be treated as final until the responsible reviewer approves it.**

If approval has not been received, clearly mark the content as:

> **DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION**

---

## Team Content Checklist

Before requesting approval:

- [ ] I read the complete existing page content
- [ ] I understand the page purpose
- [ ] I identified the main search intent
- [ ] I identified the primary topic/keyword
- [ ] I removed unnecessary repetition
- [ ] I shortened long paragraphs
- [ ] I preserved important information
- [ ] I maintained factual accuracy
- [ ] I used the correct H1/H2/H3 hierarchy
- [ ] I optimized the page for SEO
- [ ] I optimized important answers for AEO
- [ ] I improved topical clarity for GEO
- [ ] I used natural keyword variations
- [ ] I reviewed CTAs
- [ ] I reviewed internal-link opportunities
- [ ] I reviewed image ALT-text requirements
- [ ] I avoided unverified claims
- [ ] I compared the proposed content with the original
- [ ] I documented important content changes
- [ ] I submitted the content for approval
- [ ] I did not implement unapproved content

---

## Final Rule for Every Team Member

Before creating any new website page or redesigning an existing page:

> **Read → Understand → Shorten → Optimize → Compare → Review → Approve → Implement**

Never use:

> **Rewrite → Design → Publish**

without reviewing and approving the content first.
